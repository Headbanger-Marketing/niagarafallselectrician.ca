Before work begins

Give every stage a finish line of its own.

Consider a home with a driveway or parking pad and no garage. A charger or outdoor receptacle may need a weather-rated spot and a cable route from the indoor panel; settle that route in the first phase, even if the charger waits.

Write down what each phase will install, what it leaves ready for the next budget and what the home relies on in between. Ask that every phase end with labelled circuits and written records you can keep.

Is it sensible to stagger electrical jobs as money allows?

It can, if each phase is complete and safe by itself. Ask the contractor to set out the whole plan before the first stage, then to say what each stage installs and what it leaves for later.

Which records should I keep after each phase is finished?

Keep the written scope, the invoice, any ESA paperwork and photographs of the labelled panel door and the new equipment. Ask for these at the end of every phase, not only the last one.

What makes two partial quotes fair to compare side by side?

Ask each contractor to price the same defined stage and to list what is excluded. Quotes are only comparable when the scope, the equipment supplied and the finishing work are written in the same terms.

Does a home with only a driveway change where equipment goes?

It can. Without a garage, a charger or receptacle would sit outdoors, so ask about outdoor-rated equipment, the mounting position and how the cable reaches it from the indoor panel. A shared driveway, if you have one, adds access questions.
